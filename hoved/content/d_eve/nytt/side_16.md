---
title: Religious Responses to Climate Change
description: How religious communities both engage with and obstruct climate action, and why a secular, evidence-based response is needed.
---

Climate change tests every worldview, and religious responses have been mixed. Some faith communities treat environmental stewardship as a religious duty and have joined conservation efforts. Others deny climate science or treat the crisis as divine providence, delaying the action the evidence demands.

## Constructive Engagement

Many religious institutions have mobilized around creation care, linking environmental protection to moral teaching. Their networks can reach audiences that scientific institutions cannot, encouraging sustainable behavior in communities where trust in experts is low.

## Obstruction and Delay

Elsewhere, climate denial has found religious expression. When disasters are framed as divine punishment or signs of the end times, human responsibility is dismissed and mitigation efforts are undermined. Policy then waits on doctrine instead of data.

## A Secular View

Secular and humanist groups argue that climate action must rest on evidence, not belief. Cooperation with religious communities is welcome where it reduces harm, but scientific consensus cannot be overridden by faith. The planet's future belongs to everyone and demands a common, rational response.
