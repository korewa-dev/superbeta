---
title: The Global Decline of Organized Religion
description: Surveying the worldwide fall in religious belief and practice, its causes, and what the trend means for secularism and society.
---

Organized religion is in decline across much of the world. In Western Europe, church attendance has fallen for decades, and the fastest-growing religious identity in many countries is "none." The trend is visible in North America, East Asia, and increasingly among younger generations everywhere, including Muslim-majority societies where belief persists but practice is changing.

## Causes

The causes are debated but familiar: education, urbanization, science, and prosperity. As people gain security and knowledge, supernatural explanations for the world lose their grip. Scandals involving religious institutions have also driven trust away.

## Uneven Change

Decline is not uniform. Religion remains strong in parts of Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia, and some movements are revitalizing. What is changing is the default assumption that belief is normal and doubt is deviant. Apostasy, once unthinkable, is now a real option.

## Meaning

A world with less organized religion is not a world without meaning. People still seek purpose, community, and ethics; they are simply finding them elsewhere. For secularism, the decline is less a victory than an opportunity to build inclusive institutions around shared human values.
