---
title: News Media Treatment of Atheists and Secularists
description: How journalism portrays atheism and secular movements, for better and worse
---

Journalism shapes how the public understands atheism and secularism, and the coverage is often uneven. When a religious group is attacked, the story is front-page news. When an atheist is harassed, it rarely makes the papers.

## Framing the story

Atheists are frequently framed by what they are against rather than what they stand for. The word "atheist" itself can become a label that arouses suspicion, while "secular" is sometimes used as a synonym for "hostile to religion". Careless framing reinforces stigma.

## Good reporting exists

There is also genuinely strong reporting that treats nonbelief as an ordinary position: profiles of secular families, investigations of state-sponsored religious pressure, and balanced coverage of church-state debates. Such journalism helps normalise nonbelief.

## A call for accuracy

Secular groups ask reporters to apply the same standards they use for any minority: accurate labels, context, and serious engagement with ideas. When the media gets this right, the public conversation improves.
