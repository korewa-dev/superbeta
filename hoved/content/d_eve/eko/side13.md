---
title: Religious Donations and Transparency Issues
description: Why the world's least examined money — religious giving — needs audit, oversight, and honest reporting.
---

Religious donations move enormous sums around the world, yet they are often the least examined money in the economy. Zakat, tithes, temple offerings, and church donations are collected in cash, rarely audited, and usually exempt from reporting rules.

## Trust Without Oversight

Devotion makes donors reluctant to ask questions. A believer who hands money to a preacher or trustee rarely receives a receipt or a financial statement. This openness to trust becomes an opening for abuse. Scandals from American megachurches to Indian temple trusts repeatedly reveal luxury homes, private jets, and misused funds — while the faithful are told to give more.

## The Accountability Gap

Because religious organizations often enjoy tax exemptions and legal protection, governments hesitate to require transparency. That gap tempts money laundering: untraceable cash can move through charity accounts with few questions. In Bangladesh, where informal religious giving is large and undocumented, this is a genuine concern.

## Demanding Honesty

Transparency is not hostility to faith. Publishing audited accounts, separating charity from profit, and protecting whistleblowers would strengthen the very trust religion depends on. A donation system that can be audited is one believers can trust and one the public can respect.
