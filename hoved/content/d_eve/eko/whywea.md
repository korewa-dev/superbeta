---
title: Why Wealthier Societies Tend to Become More Secular
description: An evidence-based look at the strong correlation between rising incomes and declining religiosity, and the social forces that help explain it.
---

In global surveys, a consistent pattern emerges: as per-capita income rises, self-reported religiosity tends to fall. Wealthier nations such as those in Scandinavia report some of the lowest rates of regular worship, while poorer countries like Bangladesh and Nigeria record some of the highest. This does not prove that wealth causes secularism, but it points to a powerful correlation.

## Education and Existential Security

Social scientists argue that economic development reduces existential insecurity. When people have reliable healthcare, pensions, and stable food supplies, the need for supernatural assurance weakens. Education, which spreads with development, also encourages evidence-based reasoning and makes young people more likely to question inherited doctrines.

## A Correlation, Not a Law

The relationship is not automatic. Oil-rich Gulf states remain highly religious despite high incomes, and the United States is wealthier than many less observant European nations. Culture, inequality, and politics all shape the outcome. Still, across most of the world, development and secularization move together.

For secularists, this is neither a threat nor a promise, but an observation: societies that meet material needs reliably tend to turn their attention elsewhere.
