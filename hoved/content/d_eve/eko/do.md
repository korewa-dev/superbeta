---
title: Do Religious Restrictions Affect Economic Growth?
description: Evidence that restricting belief, or tying commerce to religious rules, raises costs and chases away investment.
---

Governments restrict religion in many ways — banning practices, controlling dress, taxing minority worship, or, conversely, enforcing majority religion through law. Do these restrictions hurt growth? The evidence says they often do.

## How Restrictions Bite

Restrictions raise costs and uncertainty. A business in a country where religious identity determines access to credit, licenses, or contracts allocates energy to navigating rules rather than producing. Laws that force religious minorities to pay special taxes, or that exclude them from public employment, waste talent and invite instability. ## The Research Picture

Cross-country studies find that religious discrimination correlates with lower investment, less trade, and weaker institutions. Countries that treat religious groups unequally tend to have less trust, and trust is a key input into economic exchange. Iran's efforts to purge un-Islamic business practices, or the chilling effect of blasphemy laws on investment, illustrate the pattern.

## The Growth-Friendly Path

Restricting belief is not necessary for order. Legal frameworks that protect all religions equally reduce conflict, stabilize expectations, and let markets work. Economic freedom and religious liberty, in practice, tend to rise and fall together.
