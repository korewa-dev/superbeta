---
title: The Economic Cost of Religious Extremism
description: How extremist violence and discrimination destroy livelihoods, chase away investment, and drain public budgets.
---

When religious extremism turns violent, the costs are measured not only in lives but in destroyed livelihoods. Attacks, bombings, and armed campaigns chase away investment, close factories, and empty the hotels that once employed thousands.

## Direct and Indirect Damage

The direct damage is clear: infrastructure ruined, businesses looted, and insurance claims piling up. The indirect damage is larger and slower. Capital is patient and skittish; investors avoid countries where kidnapping and extortion are routine. Skilled workers emigrate, taking their education and savings with them. Governments divert scarce funds from schools and clinics toward policing and counterterrorism.

## Bangladesh's Experience

Bangladesh has felt these pressures. After attacks on secular bloggers and minorities, the garment industry — the country's export engine — faced buyer scrutiny and higher security costs. Even small incidents scare foreign retailers.

## The Lesson

Extremism is not just a security problem; it is an economic problem with deep social roots. Treating it that way — through employment, education, and law enforcement that protects all citizens — is both humane and economically sensible. Peace is the cheapest development policy available.
