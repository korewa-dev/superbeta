---
title: The Cost of Religious Conflict
description: Why communal violence destroys trade networks, forces talent to flee, and leaves cities poorer for decades.
---

Religious conflict rarely stays spiritual. When communities turn on each other, markets are among the first casualties, and recovery takes years.

## Destroyed Trade Networks

Conflict breaks the trust on which trade depends. A trader who cannot safely cross a neighborhood divided by religion loses customers and suppliers. Communal violence in places like India or Nigeria has repeatedly burned shops, disrupted harvests, and cut off villages from regional markets. Minority businesses, most exposed, are often the first to close.

## Lost Opportunity

The opportunity cost is just as real. Cities divided by religion duplicate services — separate schools, clinics, and housing — that a united population could share cheaply. Skilled people leave, and the talent that remains is spent on self-protection rather than production.

## The Price of Peace

Peace is not merely the absence of shooting; it is the return of normal commerce. Rebuilding trust between communities is one of the most productive investments a government can make. When citizens can buy, sell, and work together freely, everyone's income rises. That is the most concrete argument for religious tolerance a secularist can offer.
