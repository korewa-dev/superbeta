---
title: Economic Development and Secularization
description: What the evidence really shows about the link between modernization, prosperity, and religious decline.
---

Does prosperity make people less religious? The famous secularization thesis says yes: as economies modernize, religious belief and practice decline. The data, though, tell a more complicated story.

## What the Evidence Shows

In most wealthy countries, church attendance and self-reported belief have fallen steadily. Northern Europe is the clearest case. Yet the United States remained strikingly religious despite high income for much of the twentieth century, and many middle-income countries have seen rising religious fervor even as they grew. Bangladesh's economy has expanded rapidly while public religiosity has if anything strengthened.

## Development Changes the Question

Development does not simply erase faith; it changes what people believe and how. Urban life, education, science, and media give people alternatives to religious explanation. Faith becomes more personal, more mobile, and often more intense — expressed through new churches and movements rather than old hierarchies.

## Beyond Simple Prediction

The honest conclusion is that development weakens some forms of religion — institutional authority, supernatural explanations of disease and weather, large families — while reshaping others. Secularization is not destiny; it is a tendency that depends on how, and how fast, societies develop. That uncertainty is itself the most interesting finding.
