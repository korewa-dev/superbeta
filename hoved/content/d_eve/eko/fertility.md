---
title: Fertility Rates, Religion, and Economic Outcomes
description: How religiously driven family size shapes demographics, investment in children, and national growth.
---

Fertility varies sharply by religion, and those differences matter for national economies. Countries where religious families are large face very different futures than those where families are small.

## The Religious Difference

Across South Asia, Muslim and Hindu families historically had more children than Christian and Sikh families, and religious leaders often discouraged birth control. Conservative religious groups worldwide oppose contraception and family planning programs, arguing that children are a blessing. In Bangladesh, fertility fell dramatically — from over six children per woman in the 1970s to about two today — as education, urbanization, and family planning spread, reshaping every religious community.

## The Economic Consequences

Falling fertility is strongly associated with rising income. Fewer children per family means more investment in each child's health and schooling, freeing women for paid work and shrinking the share of income spent on dependents. The demographic dividend — a large working-age population — can fuel growth for decades.

## Beyond the Headlines

High fertility is not simply religious people having more children. It reflects poverty, women's education, and access to contraception. When secular policies expand schooling and family planning, fertility falls and living standards rise — in religious and secular families alike.
