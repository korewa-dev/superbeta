---
title: Pilgrimage Economies Around the World
description: How sacred journeys from the Hajj to Kumbh Mela generate billions in spending, and who bears the costs.
---

Pilgrimage is one of the oldest forms of travel, and today it is big business. The Hajj alone draws millions to Mecca each year; Kumbh Mela gatherings in India count tens of millions; and Varanasi, Jerusalem, and Lourdes anchor entire regional economies.

## Spending and Infrastructure

Pilgrims need transport, lodging, food, and ritual supplies, and they spend generously because the journey is sacred. Hajj visas, flights, and hotels generate billions for Saudi Arabia. Around smaller shrines in Bangladesh, local markets, rickshaws, and guesthouses exist mainly to serve visitors during festivals. Governments invest in airports, sanitation, and crowd control, often treating pilgrims as a reliable source of revenue.

## Costs Behind the Blessing

The crowds also create costs: waste, disease, and accidents, such as the stampedes that have marred the Hajj. Land near holy sites becomes expensive, pushing out residents. Money that could reach local communities is sometimes captured by temple trusts or tour operators.

## An Honest Accounting

For secular observers, pilgrimage is a natural experiment in demand. Faith reliably moves money, and measuring those flows — who earns, who pays, and who cleans up afterward — tells us far more than devotional language about how these economies really run.
