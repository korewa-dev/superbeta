---
title: Economic Challenges Faced by Religious Minorities
description: How exclusion, insecurity, and distrust create measurable gaps in wealth, employment, and opportunity for minorities.
---

Religious minorities often face economic disadvantages that go far beyond occasional discrimination. Exclusion, insecurity, and distrust compound into measurable gaps in wealth, employment, and opportunity.

## The Forms of Disadvantage

Minorities may be denied jobs, bank loans, or housing in majority-dominated markets. Business owners may be forced to pay protection, face boycotts, or lose customers when communal tensions rise. In Bangladesh, Hindu minorities have long reported disproportionate harassment, land-grabbing, and attacks on temples, which drives families to leave — and their assets with them. Even when no law discriminates, social networks, the channel through which jobs and credit flow, favor the majority.

## The Cost of Exodus

When minorities flee, the economy loses skilled workers, traders, and taxpayers. Countries that push out or neglect their minorities forfeit the entrepreneurial energy of communities like Jews in Europe, Armenians in the Ottoman Empire, or Hindus in Pakistan — all of whom prospered where secure and left where they were not.

## Equality as Economic Policy

Protecting minorities is not charity; it is productive. Equal access to markets, courts, and schools expands the pool of talent every economy needs. A state that defends all its citizens equally gets more production from all of them.
