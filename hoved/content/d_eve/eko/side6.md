---
title: How Superstition Influences Financial Decisions
description: How lucky numbers, unlucky days, and auspicious timing put a measurable tax on real money decisions.
---

Superstition shapes real money choices. Property prices, stock trading, business timing, and even insurance markets all show measurable effects from beliefs that have nothing to do with evidence.

## Lucky Numbers, Unlucky Floors

In China, apartments with floors containing the number 4 — homophonous with death — sell at discounts of several percent, while buildings that skip unlucky floors in their numbering change the market in nearby blocks. In Western markets, Friday the 13th shows small but persistent drops in trading volume. Lottery numbers, car plates, and phone numbers all command premiums when they contain lucky digits like 8 or 9.

## Business and Ritual

Small traders in Bangladesh and across South Asia routinely consult astrologers and auspicious days before opening shops, signing contracts, or launching ventures. Delaying a deal for a good day is a real cost, and paying a guru or astrologer is a real expense.

## The Cost of Belief

Individually these costs are small; collectively they are large. Superstition taxes every transaction it touches. It does not follow that believers are foolish — uncertainty makes ritual reassuring. But naming the cost, and testing superstitions against outcomes, is exactly what financial literacy and secular education should teach.
