---
title: The Political History of Blasphemy Laws
description: How blasphemy laws evolved from protecting churches to policing offense, and the political forces that keep them alive today.
---

Blasphemy laws are among the oldest surviving restrictions on expression. In Europe they protected established churches from dissent, punishing those who questioned official doctrine. Their purpose was always political as much as theological: guarding the religious legitimacy that rulers used to secure obedience.

## The Colonial Reinvention

The British Empire exported blasphemy-style restrictions to colonial territories, where they became tools for managing majority sentiment. India's colonial penal code and later Pakistan's laws added religion-specific provisions, and Bangladesh inherited a version of the same legal machinery. These laws outlived their colonial authors and were strengthened by the very states they once served.

## Why They Persist

Blasphemy laws persist because they are politically convenient. They let governments pose as guardians of faith, silence critics under the cover of protecting harmony, and respond to mob pressure without defending the rule of law. Each episode of enforcement teaches activists that offense is a lever of power.

History shows these laws survive not because faith needs them but because governments find them useful. Repeal requires confronting that political calculus, not merely theological argument.
