---
title: Freedom of Religion vs Freedom From Religion
description: Distinguishing the right to practice a faith from the right to be free of imposed religion, and why both matter in democracies.
---

Freedom of religion protects the right to believe, worship, and practice a faith. Freedom from religion protects the right not to be subjected to religious belief against one's will. The two are often treated as opposites, but human rights law treats them as two sides of the same coin.

## Two Rights, One Principle

Both freedoms rest on the same foundation: consent. A person who can be coerced into observance is not truly free to believe. Equally, a person forced to receive religious instruction, obey religious law, or pay for religious institutions has lost the freedom to disbelieve. Protecting one without the other leaves the right incomplete.

## When They Conflict

Conflicts arise when religious practice intrudes on others, as with public proselytizing in state schools or religious dress codes imposed on employees. Secular states typically resolve these by protecting private practice while keeping state institutions neutral.

Neither freedom should swallow the other. A society that protects both treats belief as a private conviction rather than a public duty, allowing believers and non-believers to live together without either imposing on the other.
