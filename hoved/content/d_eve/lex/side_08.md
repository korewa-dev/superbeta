---
title: Religion and Censorship Laws
description: Why religiously motivated censorship laws are adopted, how they suppress dissent, and what a secular approach to expression requires.
---

Many states maintain laws that restrict expression deemed offensive to religion, including blasphemy and defamation-of-religion statutes. These laws are often presented as protecting harmony, but their real effects are political: they hand an official veto over speech to anyone who claims offense.

## The Dynamics of Offense

Because "offense" is subjective, censorship laws are easily weaponized. Governments use them to silence critics, and minorities can be targeted by false accusations. Where penalties are severe, journalists and publishers practice self-censorship, and the most provocative voices become the most prominent, crowding out reasoned debate.

## The Secular Alternative

Secular frameworks do not protect religion from criticism; they protect persons from defamation and incitement to violence, which are already criminal without special religious exemptions. Under such rules, a person may criticize beliefs freely but cannot defame or threaten a person.

History shows that religious censorship does not protect faith, which can endure criticism, but protects power from scrutiny. A consistent approach applies the same protections and limits to all speech, religious or not, without making any doctrine legally untouchable.
