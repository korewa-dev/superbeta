---
title: Apostasy Laws and Human Rights
description: An evidence-based look at apostasy laws, their human rights impact, and why secular legal frameworks reject them.
---

Apostasy laws punish people who leave a religion, typically Islam, and remain on the books in several countries across the Middle East, Africa, and South Asia. Under international law, these statutes clash with the right to freedom of thought, conscience, and religion, including the right to change one's religion, guaranteed by Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

## The Human Rights Conflict

Criminalizing apostasy treats belief as a status to be policed rather than a matter of individual conscience. Punishments can range from imprisonment to the death penalty, and even where not actively enforced, such laws fuel discrimination, intimidation, and mob violence against converts and skeptics.

## Why Secularism Matters

Secular legal systems draw a clear line between private belief and public law. They protect the freedom to believe, not believe, or change belief without state interference. This is not hostility toward religion; it is equal treatment before the law for believers and non-believers alike.

As long as apostasy laws persist, genuine freedom of conscience remains incomplete. A society that respects human rights must protect the right to exit a faith as fully as the right to enter one.
