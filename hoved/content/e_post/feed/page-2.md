---
title: Artificial Intelligence Ethics
description: Examining bias, transparency, and accountability in AI systems, and why reason and evidence should guide their design.
---

Artificial intelligence now decides credit applications, recommends medical treatments, and powers surveillance systems. Because these systems shape real lives, their design raises ethical questions that demand public scrutiny rather than blind acceptance.

## Bias and Fairness

Machine-learning models learn from past data, which can carry human prejudice. Studies show facial-recognition and hiring tools performing worse for minority groups, highlighting why datasets must be examined openly.

## Transparency and Accountability

Many models remain opaque, making errors hard to explain or challenge. Clear rules on who answers for harm, how decisions are justified, and what data is collected are essential safeguards.

## A Secular View

Ethics grounded in reason, evidence, and human well-being offers a stronger basis for AI policy than appeals to tradition. Societies benefit when citizens understand these tools and hold developers to measurable, verifiable standards of fairness and care.
