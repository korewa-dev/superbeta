---
title: How Sacred Texts Were Compiled
description: The messy, human history of how Bibles, Qurans, and other canons were edited, selected, and fixed.
---

I grew up imagining sacred books descending from heaven, perfect and whole. Then I opened the door to biblical scholarship and found something far more human: councils, debates, lost manuscripts, and editors with agendas. The texts I was told were eternal turned out to have been assembled, chosen, and revised over centuries.

## The Selection Process

The Bible did not arrive as a single book. It is a library of texts written by many hands over a thousand years, and even the church took centuries to decide which books belonged inside it. Dozens of gospels circulated in early Christianity; only four made the final cut, chosen by councils whose members argued, voted, and excluded the rest. Translators made choices that became doctrine.

## What the Process Reveals

The Quran is likewise a record of a spoken revelation gathered after the prophet's death, with companion traditions openly noting variant readings. Neither process is divine machinery. Both are human effort — sincere, but human.

This did not shatter my respect for the texts. It changed what I respected: not their claim to divine authorship, but the astonishing, flawed, creative minds that wrote and preserved them across ages. A book does not need to be the word of God to be worth reading. It only needs to be honest about what it is.
