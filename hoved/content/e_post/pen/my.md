---
title: My First Doubts About Religion
description: The small, ordinary moments that cracked a lifetime of certainty — and where they led.
---

I expected my deconversion to begin with a dramatic argument or a devastating scientific discovery. It did not. My first doubts arrived quietly, in ordinary moments, in the gap between what religion promised and what I watched happen.

## The First Cracks

I remember a faithful neighbor whose daughter was sick, and the prayers that did not work while the medicine did. I remember reading scripture closely for the first time and finding contradictions and cruelties I had been taught to skip. And I remember asking, very young, why my religion was true and the millions of others were not — and being told not to ask.

## The Question That Stayed

None of these moments was decisive alone. But they accumulated like dust until I could not see past them. The real turning point was simpler still: I realized I had never examined my beliefs the way I examined everything else. I had believed because I was born into it, and that, for me, became the least convincing reason of all.

Looking back, I am grateful for those small doubts. They were not attacks on my faith. They were my mind's first honest attempts to protect me from a lifetime of pretending. I followed them, and they led me home to the truth.
