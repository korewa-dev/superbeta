---
title: Why Prophecies Usually Fail
description: How vague predictions, hindsight interpretation and moving goalposts keep prophecy alive even when it never comes true.
---

Prophecies have an astonishing record of failure, yet belief in prophecy never dies. The explanation is not mystery; it is method. Prophecies are crafted, interpreted, and defended in ways that make failure almost invisible. Once you see the method, the track record stops being impressive.

## The Vagueness Trick

The strongest prophecies are the vaguest. Predictions that a great king will arise, that times will be dark, that a saviour will come, can be fitted to almost any century. They succeed by being unfalsifiable, not by being accurate. The few specific predictions in scripture can usually be dated to events that already happened when the text was written.

## Moving the Goalposts

When a predicted date passes, the response is rarely doubt. The date is reinterpreted, the prophecy is re-read as spiritual rather than literal, or the failure is said to be a test. Every miss becomes evidence of the believer's faithfulness rather than the prophet's error.

Given these rules, prophecy will never fail enough to be abandoned. That is precisely why it proves so little.
