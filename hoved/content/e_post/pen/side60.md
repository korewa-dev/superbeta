---
title: Women and Religious Authority Throughout History
description: How religions have excluded women from power, rewritten their scriptures to justify it, and why the exclusion is a human, not divine, invention.
---

Open almost any sacred text and you will find women's voices, prophetesses, teachers, and leaders. Walk into most of the institutions built on those texts and you will find men in the positions of authority. The gap between the record and the institution is not accidental. It was created over centuries, and it can be traced.

## The Record of Exclusion

Early Christianity had women deacons and house-church leaders before the male hierarchy tightened. Islam's early community heard women scholars, yet mosque leadership became male. The pattern repeats across religions: as institutions grew richer and more political, women were pushed out, and scriptures were interpreted to bless the outcome.

## A Human Hand

Scholars can date these shifts. The rulings that excluded women were made by men, in particular centuries, serving particular interests. They were then presented as timeless. Once we see the history, the divine veneer wears thin.

Women have always been part of religious life. What has changed is not their faith but the permission they were given. That permission was always a human decision, and it can be changed.
