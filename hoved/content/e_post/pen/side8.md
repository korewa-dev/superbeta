---
title: Famous Critics of Religion Through History
description: From Lucretius to Russell, the long lineage of honest doubters who questioned the gods and survived.
---

Atheism is not a modern invention, and it has never been a lonely one. Throughout history, people have examined the claims of religion and found them wanting — often at great personal cost. Reading their words, I felt less alone and more connected.

## The Early Voices

The Greek philosopher Epicurus asked how a good god could permit suffering, and his follower Lucretius wrote a poem explaining the world without gods entirely. In the Islamic world, thinkers like Al-Razi doubted prophecy outright. Later, Voltaire and the Enlightenment philosophes attacked priestly power with wit sharp enough to still draw blood.

## The Modern Critics

Charles Darwin supplied the deepest blow by showing we need no designer. Thomas Paine called the Bible a human book. Bertrand Russell asked how we could judge God by human standards we already possess. Today, figures like Richard Dawkins and Ayaan Hirsi Ali carry the argument into the open.

None of these critics agreed on everything. That is precisely the point: this is not a creed but a method. Each generation rediscovers, independently, that questions are better than answers we were told not to ask. I am grateful to stand in that long line.
