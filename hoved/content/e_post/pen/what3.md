---
title: What Science Can and Cannot Say About Gods
description: Science cannot measure God, but it can investigate every testable claim religion makes, and that boundary is worth understanding.
---

There is a popular idea that science and religion occupy separate rooms and never need to speak. I think that is too tidy. The real situation is more interesting: science cannot weigh a soul, but it can examine every claim a religion makes about the physical world. The boundary is real, and it is worth drawing carefully.

## What Science Cannot Do

Science cannot confirm or refute a purely abstract God who never acts in the world. If a being has no detectable effects, no experiment can find it. That is not a defeat for science; it is a definition. A god who never touches nature is, for all practical purposes, absent.

## What Science Does Do

The moment religion makes a claim about reality, science has something to say. That the earth is young. That prayers heal. That a flood covered the world. That a prophet split the sea. Each claim moves into the realm of evidence, and evidence has its own verdict.

Science does not announce the non-existence of gods. It simply holds the door of the laboratory open, and notes how few claims ever walk through it.
