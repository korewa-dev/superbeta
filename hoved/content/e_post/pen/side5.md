---
title: Can Prayer Be Scientifically Tested?
description: What controlled studies of intercessory prayer actually found, and why the results make sense.
---

If prayer worked as religion claims — changing real-world outcomes through supernatural intervention — science should be able to measure it. The remarkable thing is that scientists tried, and the answer came back clear.

## The Studies That Were Done

In the most careful trials, heart-surgery patients were randomly assigned to receive intercessory prayer or not, without knowing which group they were in. The prayed-for group showed no advantage in recovery, complications, or survival. Larger reviews reached the same conclusion: no measurable effect.

## Why That Matters

This should not surprise anyone. Prayer is not a physical force, so it has no physical lever to pull. If concentrated prayer measurably improved outcomes, hospitals would be staffed with prayer groups. What the studies cannot measure is the comfort prayer brings the person praying. That value is real, but psychological, not supernatural. Naming it honestly honors it. Belief need not be true to be soothing — but understanding is truer, and worth the trade.
