---
title: Why Humans Detect Agency Where None Exists
description: How an evolved instinct for spotting minds behind events produces both survival advantages and gods.
---

Our ancestors lived in a dangerous world where a rustle could mean a predator. The brain that assumed an agent behind the sound and fled survived; the one that waited for proof did not. Over countless generations, this hyperactive agency detection became part of us. It is why we still feel watched in empty rooms and see faces in clouds.

## A Useful Error

Most of the time, false alarms cost little and misses cost everything. The bias is expensive in the long run, though. A brain designed to find minds keeps finding them. The wind becomes a spirit, illness becomes a curse, and the universe itself becomes a being who watches and cares. This is the psychological ground on which gods grow.

## Beyond the Bias

Naming the mechanism does not settle the question of whether a god exists. But it does reframe it. The instinct to see agency is present in us whether or not there is any agent to see. That is why testimony of the divine feels so convincing and yet is so difficult to confirm.

Understanding ourselves is the beginning of wisdom, and wisdom begins with not trusting every feeling the brain manufactures.
