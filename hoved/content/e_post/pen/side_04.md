---
title: Can Miracles Be Tested?
description: Why miracle claims always collapse under scrutiny, and what a genuine, testable miracle would actually require.
---

Every religion has its miracles, and every miracle has a problem: examined carefully, it either disappears or shrinks into something ordinary. That pattern is so consistent that I came to treat "miracle" as a label we attach to events we have not yet explained.

## The Test of a Miracle

A real miracle should be testable. If prayer restored severed limbs, hospitals would not need surgery. If faith cured cancer, insurers would study believers instead of medicine. The fact that miracles are always reported in the past, far away, or secondhand — never under controlled conditions — is not coincidence. It is the signature of an unfalsifiable claim.

## When Miracles Fail

Placebo effects, spontaneous remission, misdiagnosis, and honest exaggeration explain nearly every case. The few that resist explanation prove only that we do not yet know the cause. Unknown is not supernatural; history is a graveyard of "miracles" that became ordinary once understood. I no longer ask if miracles are possible. I ask whether the claim survives a skeptical test. So far, none has.
