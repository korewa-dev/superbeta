The Books That Made Me Question Belief

A personal account of the books that gradually loosened the grip of faith and set me on a secular path.

Belief rarely falls in a single dramatic moment. For me it eroded book by book, each one posing a question my teachers could not answer. I did not set out to become an atheist. I set out to read honestly, and the reading took me somewhere I had not planned to go.

The Bible Itself

The most decisive volume was the one I was told was perfect. Reading it cover to cover, without the filter of sermons, I found contradictions, violent passages, and two very different versions of God. Scholars later showed me the Bible was written by many hands over centuries, edited and selected by councils.

Sceptics and Historians

From there came the essayists who asked why we believe at all, and the historians who traced how religions actually develop, grow, and borrow from one another. Each page made the supernatural seem less like a revelation and more like a product of human culture.

By the end I had not lost my sense of wonder. I had simply stopped giving it a divine address.
