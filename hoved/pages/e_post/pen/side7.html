Faith Healing Under Scientific Examination

A look at what faith healing is, the harms it causes, and why its successes vanish under scrutiny.

Faith healing promises what medicine cannot: restoration through belief alone. When I began examining these claims, I expected to find fraud. What I found was more interesting — and more dangerous.

When Healing Becomes Harm

The pattern is consistent. A sick person stops treatment, prayers are offered, and a recovery is announced. The quiet tragedies rarely make the news: the diabetic child who died without insulin, the cancer patient who delayed surgery until too late. Every genuine-sounding case, followed to its source, involves misdiagnosis, spontaneous remission, or recovery already begun. None has outperformed placebo in a controlled test.

The Real Price

I do not accuse every healer of deliberate deception; many sincerely believe. But sincerity does not heal infections, and good intentions do not excuse lost children. When faith is treated as medicine, faith becomes a risk factor. Medicine earns trust by being tested. Faith healing asks for trust by refusing to be tested — and that is the whole problem in one sentence.
