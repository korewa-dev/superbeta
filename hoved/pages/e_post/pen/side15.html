How Political Leaders Used Religion Throughout History

The oldest alliance in the book — thrones and altars — and how power has always borrowed divine authority.

There is no political trick older than claiming God is on your side. From the pharaohs who were themselves gods to modern leaders who wrap themselves in scripture, the pattern is identical: those who hold power use religion to make their rule look inevitable.

Thrones and Altars

Constantine converted an empire and chose its religion — deciding what Christians would believe rather than submitting to it. The Catholic Church crowned emperors and popes, each sanctifying the other's rule. Caliphs, sultans, and kings have all presented themselves as chosen, making rebellion not just treason but heresy. When obedience to a ruler is framed as obedience to God, ordinary people bear a double burden.

The Same Playbook Today

The playbook has not changed. Elections are won by invoking faith, laws are justified by scripture, and critics are dismissed as enemies of God. The tools are ancient but always effective, because they move the argument beyond debate and into the realm of the sacred.

Seeing this history changed me. I no longer treat a leader's piety as a virtue; I treat it as a red flag and a reminder. Whenever religion sanctifies power, the powerless pay. Secular governance is not godless government. It is the only system where no ruler can claim to be speaking for God.
