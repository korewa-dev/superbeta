The Most Difficult Conversation I Had About Religion

A personal account of telling my mother I no longer believed, and what the honesty cost and gave us both.

It happened in the kitchen, over tea that neither of us drank. She had asked why I had stopped fasting, and for once I did not make an excuse. I told her the truth: I no longer believed. The silence that followed was the hardest part of the whole conversation.

The Weight of Words

She did not shout. That would have been easier. She asked, quietly, whether I had ever really believed, whether I thought she had failed as a parent, whether she would see me in the afterlife. I had answers for none of it. I could only say that I had not chosen this, that it had chosen me.

An Unfinished Peace

In the end we reached something between agreement and surrender. She still prays for me. I still love her. The conversation did not fix anything, and it did not break anything either. It just made the distance honest.

I have had many debates about religion. Only one of them mattered, and it was the one where winning was never the point.
