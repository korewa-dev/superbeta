The Invention of Religious Traditions

How many sacred practices and texts were crafted at specific times by identifiable people, not passed down untouched from antiquity.

Sacred traditions are presented as ancient, unchanged, and directly from God. Historians keep finding otherwise. Again and again, what is called age-old turns out to be a recent invention, created by identifiable people for identifiable purposes, then dressed up as timeless.

Made by Human Hands

The modern Christmas card, the shape of religious holidays, the canon of the Bible, even many supposedly ancient rituals can be dated. Books like the Book of Mormon were written in living memory. The Hebrew Bible was compiled over centuries. Credal formulas were settled at councils whose minutes survive.

Why It Matters

This matters because authority rests on antiquity. A tradition is easier to obey when it seems to come from a remote, unquestionable past. When we see the assembly line, the spell breaks. We realise the tradition was made by people with the same doubts and motives we have.

Discovering the human origin of a tradition is not discovering that it is worthless. It is discovering that its value depends on its content, not its age.
