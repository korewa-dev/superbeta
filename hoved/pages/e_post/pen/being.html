Being Openly Nonreligious in Public

A personal reflection on the costs and rewards of saying plainly that you do not believe, especially in a religious society.

I did not plan to announce my atheism. It arrived the way most honesty does — gradually, then all at once. The first time I said "I don't believe" out loud, my voice shook. The second time, it did not. Somewhere between them I realized the fear was not about God at all. It was about people.

What It Costs

Being openly nonreligious means losing the easy assumption of belonging. Family gatherings grow complicated. Strangers ask pointed questions. In places where faith is woven into law and custom, the price can be real — jobs, marriages, even safety. I do not pretend those costs are small.

What It Returns

What it returns is larger. A life without performance. Conversations where I no longer measure my words against a scripture. Children who grow up knowing their parents' beliefs are their own. Each time I speak plainly, I make it slightly easier for the next person to do the same.

Secrecy protects us, but honesty builds community. I have found that most people — religious or not — respect a calm, kind voice more than a silent one. That is why I keep speaking.
