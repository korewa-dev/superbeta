How Religions Borrowed From Earlier Religions

How flood myths, virgin births, and resurrection stories circulated across cultures long before any sacred canon existed.

Most believers assume their religion arrived complete and original. But when scholars compare the texts, an obvious pattern emerges: religions borrow constantly from one another. The sacred books of the present are full of stories that were already ancient when those books were written.

The Shared Stories

The flood myth appears in Mesopotamia centuries before Noah. Resurrection narratives surround many dying-and-rising gods — Osiris, Tammuz, Mithras. Virgin births, divine sons, and cosmic battles between good and evil circulate across the region like weather. Judaism absorbed Babylonian myths during exile; Christianity grew up inside Hellenistic and Roman culture, adopting their language and categories; Islam, in turn, builds directly on both.

Why This Matters

This is not an attack. It is simply history. Every culture writes its deepest hopes into its sacred stories, and stories, being human, always borrow. Seeing the threads does not require dismissing the tapestries — but it does require admitting the seams.

For me, the borrowing is evidence of something wonderful: that our ancestors were curious, connected, and creative. What it is not is evidence of a uniquely revealed truth. If every scripture echoes the ones before it, perhaps they are all human responses to the same questions — not answers delivered from above.
