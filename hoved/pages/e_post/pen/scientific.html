Scientific Explanations for Near-Death Experiences

Why vivid visions during cardiac arrest are better explained by brain chemistry than by proof of an afterlife.

People who have near-death experiences describe tunnels of light, a sense of floating, and meetings with deceased relatives. For many, these are taken as evidence that the soul survives death. But the details point somewhere else entirely.

What Happens in the Brain

During cardiac arrest, blood flow to the brain collapses and consciousness fades. Yet researchers have documented patients who report lucid experiences during resuscitation. The leading explanations involve oxygen deprivation, the release of endorphins and DMT-like compounds, and abnormal electrical activity as the cortex shuts down. The tunnel of light may be a widening loss of peripheral vision. The floating sensation matches what happens when the brain misinterprets body signals.

An Honest Conclusion

None of this proves there is no afterlife. It proves that the experience itself has natural causes that we can study and repeat. A dying brain produces a very specific, predictable set of hallucinations. The burden falls on anyone who claims otherwise to show that the light is more than chemistry.
