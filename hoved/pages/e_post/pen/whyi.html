Why I Stopped Believing

A personal essay on the questions, doubts and evidence that gradually ended my faith.

I was raised to believe, the way children are raised to speak their mother tongue. Prayer, fasting, scripture, community. For years belief was simply the air I breathed, and I never thought to examine it. When I finally did, the examination ended in a way I had never expected. I did not lose my faith in a storm. I let it go, piece by piece, in the quiet light of questions.

The Questions That Refused to Settle

Why did so many religions make the same kinds of claims? Why did the holy books read like human documents, with all their contradictions and borrowed stories? Why did prayers work for some and fail for others, with no pattern I could see? Every answer I received relied on faith, and faith was the very thing in question.

The Turning Point

At some point I realised I had stopped believing and was merely maintaining the habit. Honesty took over from there. What remained was not emptiness but relief: the world was finally big and real, and I was free to meet it as it is.
