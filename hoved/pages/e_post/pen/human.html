Human Origins: Science vs Sacred Narratives

Comparing creation myths with the fossil record, and why the evidence of evolution is more wonderful than the legend.

Every culture carries a story about where we came from — molded from clay, born of gods, sprung fully formed from the earth. The sacred narratives are beautiful, and the scientific one is true. It is also, I think, the more beautiful of the two once you actually read it.

What the Bones Say

Fossils give us a family tree written in bone. Chimpanzees share roughly ninety-eight percent of our DNA. Hominin ancestors walked upright millions of years before our species existed, and our own kind emerged in Africa some three hundred thousand years ago. There was no single first human; there was a slow, branching river of change.

A Better Kind of Wonder

The sacred stories ask us to accept that we were made in a single act. The scientific account shows something harder to accept and richer to believe: that we are cousins to every living thing, carrying the marks of ancient seas and stars in our cells. We were not placed here. We arose here, the products of billions of years of trial and error.

I do not feel diminished by my ape ancestry. I feel amazed by it. To be the first animal that can understand its own origins is not a fall from grace. It is a gift the myths never imagined.
