Leaving Superstition Behind

A personal reflection on shedding luck, omens, and evil-eye rituals — and finding the world easier to understand.

Growing up, superstition was everywhere: good days and bad days, black cats, salt over shoulders, amulets against the evil eye, and careful words to avoid tempting fate. None of it harmed me directly. But all of it occupied space in my mind that reason should have owned.

The Cost of the Small Beliefs

Superstition looks harmless next to grand doctrine, yet it works the same way. It blames misfortunes on invisible forces instead of causes we can address, so it steals agency. If your sickness is a curse, why see a doctor? If your failure is fate, why try again? Every little omen was a small surrender of my control over my own life.

Trading Comfort for Clarity

Letting go was surprisingly difficult, because superstitions are sticky by design. They reward us with tiny reassurances when we perform them. The remedy was learning that correlation is not cause, and that randomness needs no explanation. A plane is not more likely to crash because I had a bad dream.

Now I walk into uncertain days unarmed — no charms, no rituals, no lucky numbers. I find it lighter. When something goes wrong, I look for real causes and real fixes. That is not less magic in the world. It is more power in my hands.
