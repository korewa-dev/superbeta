Ocean Pollution Crisis

The scale of plastic waste and chemical runoff in the seas, and the evidence-based solutions that could reverse the damage.

The world's oceans absorb the consequences of human consumption. Plastic waste, chemical runoff, and rising acidity now threaten marine life, coastal economies, and food chains that billions of people depend on.

The Scale of the Problem

Researchers estimate that millions of tonnes of plastic enter the sea each year. Rivers carry much of it from land, while discarded fishing gear entangles wildlife. Microplastics have been found in fish, salt, and even drinking water.

What Can Be Done

Reducing single-use plastics, improving waste collection, and investing in recycling infrastructure help at the source. International agreements aim to curb plastic production and hold polluters accountable.

A Shared Responsibility

Ocean health is a scientific and moral issue that crosses borders. Evidence-based action, not denial, offers the best hope of restoring these vital ecosystems for future generations.
