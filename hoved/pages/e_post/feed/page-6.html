Digital Privacy Basics

Practical steps for controlling your personal information online and understanding the threats to digital autonomy.

Every online interaction leaves a trail. Websites track browsing, apps harvest location, and data brokers assemble profiles from public and purchased information. Understanding privacy basics helps people protect themselves.

Key Threats

Password reuse allows breaches to cascade across accounts. Free services often fund themselves by selling behavioral data. Public Wi-Fi can expose traffic to nearby attackers, while social media platforms infer sensitive traits from ordinary posts and likes.

Practical Defenses

Use unique passwords with a password manager, enable two-factor authentication, and keep software updated. Limit app permissions and review privacy settings on social platforms.

Why It Matters

Privacy is not secrecy; it is the right to control information about oneself. In an era of surveillance, targeted manipulation, and identity theft, informed digital habits are a rational safeguard for personal autonomy and dignity.
