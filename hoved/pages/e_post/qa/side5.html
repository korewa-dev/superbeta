History of Silk Road

For over a thousand years the Silk Road connected China, Central Asia and Europe, moving not only silk and spices but also ideas, religions and technology across civilisations.

For more than a millennium, the Silk Road linked China with Central Asia, the Middle East and Europe. Despite the name, it was not a single road but a shifting network of trade routes across deserts and mountains. Alongside silk, spices and gold travelled paper-making, printing and the compass.

More Than Merchandise

The exchange was cultural as well as commercial. Buddhism spread from India to East Asia, while astronomy and mathematics moved in both directions. Religious traditions encountered each other along the way, and the marketplace encouraged a practical tolerance of difference that was rare in other settings.

A Lasting Legacy

The Silk Road declined with maritime trade and later empires, but its effects shaped the modern world. Ideas exchanged along it became the foundations of science and global commerce. Historians now treat it less as a romance and more as evidence of how interconnected human civilisation has always been — a useful reminder that isolation has never been the default condition of our species.
