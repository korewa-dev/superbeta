Current Debates on Religious Education

A look at ongoing arguments over religious content in schools and universities

School classrooms have become a battleground in the debate over religious education. The central question is simple: should publicly funded schools teach religion as fact, or teach about religion as a subject?

The curriculum question

Many systems still treat the majority faith's scriptures as the starting point for moral education. Critics argue that this disadvantages minority children and excludes nonreligious students. Some countries have introduced alternatives, such as ethics and humanism classes, taught alongside or instead of religion.

What evidence shows

Studies suggest that teaching about religion — its history, texts, and practices — improves religious literacy without imposing belief. In contrast, devotional instruction tends to deepen division rather than understanding.

The way forward

A secular curriculum is not an attack on faith. It is a commitment to giving every child accurate knowledge and the freedom to make up their own mind.
