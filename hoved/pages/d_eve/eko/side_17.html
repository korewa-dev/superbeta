The Business of Faith Healing

How faith healing operates as an unregulated market in health claims, and why the sick pay the highest price.

Faith healing is a worldwide industry. From the healing churches of Lagos to traveling preachers in Dhaka and televangelists on satellite channels, practitioners sell hope to the sick and desperate.

How the Model Works

The business model is simple and powerful. A healer claims direct access to divine power and asks for a seed offering or donation, often before the prayer is made. Success is claimed loudly; failure is explained away as a lack of faith on the patient's part. Because the sick are the most hopeful, they are also the most vulnerable customers.

The Health Cost

The harm is not just financial. People skip clinics, stop taking medicine, or delay cancer surgery while they try prayer and holy water. The money spent on rituals is money not spent on doctors. In Bangladesh, where out-of-pocket health costs are already high, this can mean the difference between treatment and suffering.

A Secular Response

None of this denies the comfort of belief. But public policy should treat faith healing as what it is: an unregulated market in health claims. Evidence-based medicine, honest regulation, and health education protect everyone — especially those most in need.
